Where It Started

Robert Webb started collecting before he could read the stats on the back of the card. It was his grandfather who handed him his first one — and from that moment, the hobby became part of how he understood the world.

When his mother worked at KB Toys, Pokémon entered the picture. A new kind of card. A new community forming in real time. Robert was in the middle of it.

He fell in love not just with the cards themselves, but with what they represented: a way to get closer to something you love. A baseball card isn't just cardboard — it's a piece of a player, a moment, a season. It lets you connect to something bigger than yourself.

At age ten, Robert organized Columbia's first unofficial trade night — at a Burger King.

Kids showed up. Cards were traded. Something started.

"You can get closer to the things you love. That's what a card gives you."

The Heart of It

Sports fandom is a kind of magic. People talk about their team like they're part of it — "we won," "we lost." They're not on the field. But they feel it just the same.

Collecting is how you get even closer. A signed jersey. A rookie card from the year everything changed. A graded PSA 10 of your favorite player. These things let you hold a piece of something that matters to you.

And they let you meet people. That's always been the real point. Cards are a language. Walk into a room with a hobby box and you'll have a conversation. Show someone a rookie pull and they'll tell you their story. That's why Robert started organizing trade nights as a kid, and that's why he opened this shop.

Why This Shop Exists

Columbia deserved better.

There was no card shop in Columbia that treated customers like they actually mattered. No one who would explain why a card was worth what it was worth. No one who encouraged you to do your own research before deciding to sell. No one who was willing to lose a deal rather than take advantage of someone who didn't know what they had.

Columbia Collectibles was built to be that place. Not just a store — a resource. A community hub. Somewhere it's completely okay to come in, ask questions, and leave without buying anything.

That's still what it is today. Come in. Talk cards. Stay as long as you want. We're not going anywhere.
